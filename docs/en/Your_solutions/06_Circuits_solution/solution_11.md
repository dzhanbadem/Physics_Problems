To calculate the power and energy for a resistive circuit, we use the fundamental relationships between voltage, resistance, time, and electrical work.

### Given Information

* **Resistance ($R$):** $100 \, \Omega$
* **Voltage ($V$):** $50 \, \text{V}$
* **Time ($\Delta t$):** $5 \, \text{minutes} = 5 \times 60 \, \text{seconds} = 300 \, \text{s}$

---

### 1. Power Dissipated ($P$)

The power dissipated by a resistor is given by the formula $P = \frac{V^2}{R}$.

**Calculation:**


$$P = \frac{(50 \, \text{V})^2}{100 \, \Omega}$$

$$P = \frac{2500 \, \text{V}^2}{100 \, \Omega} = 25 \, \text{W}$$

---

### 2. Energy Consumed ($W$)

Energy (or work) is the product of power and the time interval over which it is dissipated: $W = P \cdot \Delta t$.

**Calculation:**


$$W = 25 \, \text{W} \times 300 \, \text{s}$$

$$W = 7500 \, \text{J}$$
