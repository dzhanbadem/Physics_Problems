To find the average current, we use the definition of current as the total charge transferred divided by the time interval over which the transfer occurs.

### Given Information

* **Total Charge ($Q$):** $30 \, \text{C}$
* **Time Interval ($\Delta t$):** $2 \, \text{ms} = 2 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{s} = 0.002 \, \text{s}$

---

### Concept & Formula

The average current ($I_{\text{avg}}$) is the rate of flow of charge:


$$I_{\text{avg}} = \frac{Q}{\Delta t}$$

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Set up the calculation**
Substitute the given values into the formula:


$$I_{\text{avg}} = \frac{30 \, \text{C}}{0.002 \, \text{s}}$$

**Step 2: Calculate the result**


$$I_{\text{avg}} = \frac{30}{2 \times 10^{-3}} \, \text{A}$$

$$I_{\text{avg}} = 15 \times 10^3 \, \text{A}$$

$$I_{\text{avg}} = 15,000 \, \text{A}$$

### Final Answer

The average current of the lightning bolt is **$15,000 \, \text{A}$** (or $15 \, \text{kA}$).
