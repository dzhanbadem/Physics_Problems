To find the total charge and energy stored in capacitors connected in parallel, we first determine the equivalent capacitance and then apply the standard definitions for capacitors.

### Given Information

* **Capacitor 1 ($C_1$):** $4\,\mu\text{F} = 4 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}$
* **Capacitor 2 ($C_2$):** $6\,\mu\text{F} = 6 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}$
* **Voltage ($V$):** $10\,\text{V}$

---

### 1. Total Capacitance ($C_{\text{eq}}$)

For capacitors connected in **parallel**, the equivalent capacitance is the sum of the individual capacitances:


$$C_{\text{eq}} = C_1 + C_2$$

$$C_{\text{eq}} = 4\,\mu\text{F} + 6\,\mu\text{F} = 10\,\mu\text{F} = 10 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}$$

---

### 2. Total Charge Stored ($Q_{\text{total}}$)

The total charge stored in the parallel network is calculated using the relationship $Q = C_{\text{eq}}V$:


$$Q_{\text{total}} = (10 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}) \times 10\,\text{V}$$

$$Q_{\text{total}} = 100 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{C} = 100\,\mu\text{C}$$

---

### 3. Total Energy Stored ($W$)

The total energy stored in the capacitors is given by the formula $W = \frac{1}{2} C_{\text{eq}} V^2$:


$$W = \frac{1}{2} \times (10 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}) \times (10\,\text{V})^2$$

$$W = 0.5 \times (10 \times 10^{-6}) \times 100$$

$$W = 500 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{J} = 5 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{J} = 0.5\,\text{mJ}$$

---

### Summary of Results

* **Total Charge:** $100\,\mu\text{C}$
* **Total Energy:** $0.5\,\text{mJ}$
