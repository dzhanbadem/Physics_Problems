To solve this problem, we use the transformer equations, which relate the number of turns, voltage, and current between the primary and secondary coils.

### Given Information

* **Primary turns ($N_p$):** 1000
* **Secondary turns ($N_s$):** 200
* **Primary voltage ($V_p$):** 120 V
* **Secondary current ($I_s$):** 3 A

---

### 1. Secondary Voltage ($V_s$)

The ratio of the voltages is equal to the ratio of the number of turns:


$$\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

Rearranging to solve for $V_s$:


$$V_s = V_p \times \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

$$V_s = 120 \, \text{V} \times \frac{200}{1000}$$

$$V_s = 120 \, \text{V} \times 0.2 = 24 \, \text{V}$$


**Result:** The secondary voltage is **$24 \, \text{V}$**.

---

### 2. Primary Current ($I_p$)

Assuming an ideal transformer (no power loss, where $P_p = P_s$), the ratio of currents is inversely proportional to the ratio of the turns:


$$\frac{I_p}{I_s} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

Rearranging to solve for $I_p$:


$$I_p = I_s \times \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

$$I_p = 3 \, \text{A} \times \frac{200}{1000}$$

$$I_p = 3 \, \text{A} \times 0.2 = 0.6 \, \text{A}$$


**Result:** The primary current is **$0.6 \, \text{A}$**.
