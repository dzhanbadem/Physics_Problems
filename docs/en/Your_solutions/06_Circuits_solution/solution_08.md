To determine the voltage equation for an AC circuit containing only a resistor, we use **Ohm’s Law** in its instantaneous form.

### Given Information

* **Current Equation:** $I(t) = I_{\text{max}} \sin(\omega t) = 2 \sin(120\pi t) \, \text{A}$
* **Resistance ($R$):** $50 \, \Omega$
* **Maximum Current ($I_{\text{max}}$):** $2 \, \text{A}$
* **Angular Frequency ($\omega$):** $120\pi \, \text{rad/s}$

---

### Concepts & Formulas

1. **Ohm's Law for AC:** For a purely resistive circuit, the voltage and current are **in phase**. The relationship between instantaneous voltage and current is:

$$V(t) = I(t) \cdot R$$


2. **Voltage Amplitude:** The maximum voltage ($V_{\text{max}}$) is calculated as:

$$V_{\text{max}} = I_{\text{max}} \cdot R$$



---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Calculate the maximum voltage ($V_{\text{max}}$)**
Using the peak current and the resistance:


$$V_{\text{max}} = 2 \, \text{A} \times 50 \, \Omega = 100 \, \text{V}$$

**Step 2: Formulate the voltage equation ($V(t)$)**
Since the voltage is in phase with the current in a resistive circuit, the angular frequency and the phase angle remain the same as in the current equation:


$$V(t) = V_{\text{max}} \sin(\omega t)$$

$$V(t) = 100 \sin(120\pi t) \, \text{V}$$

### Final Answer

The equation for the voltage across the resistor is:


$$V(t) = 100 \sin(120\pi t) \, \text{V}$$
