To solve this problem, we will use **Snell’s Law**, which describes how light bends as it passes between media with different refractive indices.

### Given Information

* **Index of refraction of air ($n_1$):** $1.00$
* **Index of refraction of glass ($n_2$):** $1.50$
* **Angle of incidence ($\theta_1$):** $30^\circ$
* **Target:** Angle of refraction ($\theta_2$)

### Concepts & Formulas

1. **Snell’s Law:** 
$$n_1 \sin(\theta_1) = n_2 \sin(\theta_2)$$



This formula dictates that when light enters a denser medium ($n_2 > n_1$), the ray bends **toward the normal**.

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Set up the equation**
Using the values provided:


$$1.00 \cdot \sin(30^\circ) = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)$$

**Step 2: Calculate the known side**
We know that $\sin(30^\circ) = 0.5$:


$$1.00 \cdot 0.5 = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)$$

$$0.5 = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)$$

**Step 3: Solve for $\sin(\theta_2)$**


$$\sin(\theta_2) = \frac{0.5}{1.50}$$

$$\sin(\theta_2) = 0.3333$$

**Step 4: Find the angle $\theta_2$**
To find the angle, take the inverse sine ($\arcsin$) of the result:


$$\theta_2 = \arcsin(0.3333)$$

$$\theta_2 \approx 19.47^\circ$$

### Final Answer

The angle of refraction is approximately **$19.5^\circ$**. As expected when moving into a more optically dense medium, the light ray bends toward the normal (the angle decreases from $30^\circ$ to $19.5^\circ$).
