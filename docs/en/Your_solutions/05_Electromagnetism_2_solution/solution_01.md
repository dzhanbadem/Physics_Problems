To solve this problem, we will use Gauss's Law, which relates the electric flux through a closed surface to the charge enclosed by that surface.

### Given Information

* **Enclosed Charge ($q_{\text{enc}}$):** $+2.0\text{ C}$
* **Surface Geometry:** A spherical surface of radius $r = 1.0\text{ m}$
* **Location:** The charge is at the origin, which is the center of the sphere.
* **Target:** Calculate the net electric flux ($\Phi_E$) through the spherical surface.

### Concepts & Formulas

1. **Gauss's Law:** The net electric flux $\Phi_E$ through any closed surface is equal to the net charge enclosed ($q_{\text{enc}}$) divided by the permittivity of free space ($\varepsilon_0$):

$$\Phi_E = \oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$$


* $\varepsilon_0 \approx 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}^2/(\text{N m}^2)$



### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Identify the enclosed charge**
Since the charge is at the origin and the sphere is centered at the origin with a radius of $1\text{ m}$, the entire charge of $+2\text{ C}$ is inside the spherical surface.


$$q_{\text{enc}} = +2.0\text{ C}$$

**Step 2: Apply Gauss's Law**
Using the formula $\Phi_E = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$, we substitute the known values:


$$\Phi_E = \frac{2.0\text{ C}}{8.854 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}^2/(\text{N m}^2)}$$

**Step 3: Perform the calculation**


$$\Phi_E \approx 2.259 \times 10^{11}\text{ N m}^2/\text{C}$$

*Note: The radius of the sphere ($1\text{ m}$) does not appear in the final formula. This is because, according to Gauss's Law, the total flux depends only on the charge enclosed, not the size or shape of the surface.*

The electric flux through the spherical surface is approximately **$2.26 \times 10^{11}\text{ N m}^2/\text{C}$**.
