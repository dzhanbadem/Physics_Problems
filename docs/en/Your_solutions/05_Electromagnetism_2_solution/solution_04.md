To solve this problem, we will use the formula for the magnetic torque exerted on a current-carrying loop in a uniform magnetic field.

### Given Information

* **Dimensions of the loop:** $l = 10\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ m}$, $w = 5\text{ cm} = 0.05\text{ m}$.
* **Area of the loop ($A$):** $A = l \times w = 0.1\text{ m} \times 0.05\text{ m} = 0.005\text{ m}^2$.
* **Current ($I$):** $2.0\text{ A}$.
* **Magnetic Field ($B$):** $0.3\text{ T}$.
* **Angle ($\theta$):** The magnetic field is parallel to the plane of the loop. In the torque formula, $\theta$ is defined as the angle between the **normal vector** of the loop and the magnetic field. Since the field is parallel to the plane, it is perpendicular to the normal vector. Therefore, $\theta = 90^\circ$.

### Concepts & Formulas

1. **Magnetic Torque ($\tau$):** The torque on a current loop in a magnetic field is:

$$\tau = N I A B \sin\theta$$


* $N$ is the number of turns (assumed to be 1 here).
* $\theta$ is the angle between the magnetic field vector and the area vector (the normal to the loop).



### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Determine the angle $\theta$**
The area vector (normal) is perpendicular to the plane of the loop. If the magnetic field is *parallel* to the plane, the angle between the magnetic field and the area vector is exactly $90^\circ$.


$$\sin(90^\circ) = 1$$

**Step 2: Calculate the magnetic moment ($\mu = NIA$)**
Since $N=1$:


$$\mu = I \times A = 2.0\text{ A} \times 0.005\text{ m}^2 = 0.01\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2$$

**Step 3: Calculate the Torque ($\tau$)**
Substitute the values into the torque equation:


$$\tau = \mu B \sin\theta$$

$$\tau = (0.01\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2) \times (0.3\text{ T}) \times \sin(90^\circ)$$

$$\tau = 0.003\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

### Final Answer

The magnitude of the magnetic torque on the loop is **$0.003\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$**.
